# Papadopoulos geometry bridges for the Buliga notes

This note is deliberately narrow. Papadopoulos and Buliga are **not** being merged into one program. The overlap worth recording is a set of geometric questions about metrics, distortion, local comparison, projective constructions and non-Euclidean geometry.

## Strongest bridge: Hilbert / Funk / Finsler geometry

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, “On Hilbert's fourth problem.”** arXiv:1312.3172, math.HO.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.3172
  Hilbert's fourth problem asks for metrics whose geodesics are projective line segments. Papadopoulos emphasizes the Busemann line of solutions. This is a useful historical counterpoint to Buliga's attempt to recover geometry from primitive local operations rather than assuming a smooth linear model.

Technical companions:

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, Marc Troyanov, “Weak Finsler structures and the Funk weak metric.”**
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004109002512
- **Athanase Papadopoulos, Sumio Yamada, “Timelike Hilbert and Funk geometries.”**
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.difgeo.2019.101554

The useful comparison question is not “are these dilation structures?” They are not. It is: **which parts of a metric geometry are primitive, and which are reconstructed from comparison, convexity, infinitesimal data, or allowed maps?**

## Distortion as a local geometric observable

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, “Nicolas-Auguste Tissot: A link between cartography and quasiconformal theory.”** arXiv:1612.00279, math.HO.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00279

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, “Quasiconformal mappings, from Ptolemy's geography to the work of Teichmüller.”** arXiv:1702.03756, math.HO / math.CV / math.DG / math.GT.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03756

Tissot's infinitesimal circle-to-ellipse picture is a particularly clean visualization target beside dilation structures: both ask what a local operation does under change of scale, but they encode different structures. Keeping the distinction explicit makes the comparison useful.

## Non-Euclidean geometry before modern metric language

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, “On Lobachevsky's trigonometric formulae.”** arXiv:1311.6034, math.HO.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6034

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, Guillaume Théret, “Hyperbolic geometry in the work of Johann Heinrich Lambert.”** arXiv:1503.01865.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.01865

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, Guillaume Théret, “Johann Heinrich Lambert's memoir ‘Theorie der Parallellinien’: A review with commentary.”** arXiv:2602.08420, math.HO.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08420

Buliga's notes repeatedly ask what “space” can mean without importing the whole Euclidean vector-space package. Lambert and Lobachevsky are historically useful for the same reason at a different level: they show geometry being rebuilt after weakening a supposedly structural Euclidean axiom.

## Possible experiment

Put three local visualizations beside one another:

1. Euclidean dilation: circles remain circles under isotropic scale.
2. Tissot/quasiconformal differential: circles become ellipses, recording anisotropic infinitesimal distortion.
3. A non-Euclidean / dilation-structure example: local zoom converges toward a tangent object that need not be a Euclidean vector space.

The point would be comparison, not unification.
